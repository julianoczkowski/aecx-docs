Alignments and chainage

An alignment is the line a road, railway or pipeline is set out from: its route on plan and its levels along the way. Distances along it are chainages (stations), written km 1+240.5: 1 km and 240.5 m from the zero of the line. A point off the line has an offset, 3.2 m L or 3.2 m R in the direction of travel.

Bring in an alignment

Section titled “Bring in an alignment”

Upload the model that carries it, on the Models page or by dropping files in the viewer.

  • IFC 4.3. Every IfcAlignment in the file is read with its horizontal and vertical geometry: straights, arcs, clothoids, gradients and vertical curves. The start chainage comes from the referent at the start of the line (Pset_Stationing). Further referents with their own chainage are station equations, where the chainage jumps.
  • LandXML (.xml or .landxml, versions 1.0 to 1.2), exported from Civil 3D, OpenRoads, 12d or Trimble Business Center. The file is uploaded like a model and gets versions like one.
    • Its <Alignment> elements become lines. Heights come from the design profile, and the start chainage from staStart.
    • Its TIN <Surface> elements become see-through ground models in 3D.
    • Coordinates are taken as the project’s grid (its coordinate system), so set that first.

The processing log of each version says how many alignments were found. When the same alignment comes from more than one file, the project keeps one, preferring the IFC.

Note

Models processed before alignments were supported show none. Reprocess the version on the Models page to read them.

See the line in 3D

Section titled “See the line in 3D”

When the project has an alignment, the viewer toolbar gets an Alignment button, and the line is drawn on the models:

  • a ribbon along the line;
  • a tick every 100 m;
  • a label at every kilometre.

Hold the pointer over the scheme and a readout at the bottom of the view shows where you are:

  • the chainage;
  • the offset left or right of the line;
  • the height above or below the line;
  • the alignment’s name.

The Alignment menu:

  • Show the alignments in 3D: turn the line off or on. This applies only to you.
  • Work on: when the project has more than one alignment, choose the one the chainage and the tools use.
  • Go to chainage: type 1+240 or 1240 and press Enter. The camera flies to that point on the line, looking along it.
  • The tools along the line: long section, cross section, clearance check, cut and fill and as-built vs design.

Issues and RFIs by chainage

Section titled “Issues and RFIs by chainage”

Every issue and RFI in a project with an alignment has a place on it. AECX works it out each time it is shown, so it follows the current alignment:

  1. from the issue’s pin, when it has one;
  2. otherwise from its viewpoint: the point on the camera’s line of sight closest to the alignment, within 300 m.

Photos with a position are located the same way.

The Issues and RFIs lists gain a Chainage column, with the offset beside it. On the Issues list:

  • km from … to … keeps the issues on a stretch of the line;
  • along the line sorts them by chainage, the order you walk or drive the scheme.

Ask the assistant

Section titled “Ask the assistant”

The assistant knows the line too. Ask in the words you would use on site:

  • Open issues between km 0 and km 1
  • What is open on the bridge, 1+600 to 1+800?
  • RFIs past 2+000

It answers with each item’s chainage and offset, and every item opens in 3D. In a project with an alignment, the suggestions on the assistant’s start page are questions like these.

Agents connected through MCP get the same tool, along_alignment.
