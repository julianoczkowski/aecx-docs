Bots

A bot is a teammate on a project with a standing job: chasing RFIs, triaging clashes, checking file names, writing the morning brief. You hand it work in its own conversation, it does that work with the tools it was given, and its routines run without anyone asking. Anything that would change the project waits on a card for a person to confirm.

Each bot has:

  • a job and instructions, in your words;
  • its tools: what it may look up, what it may prepare for you, and what it may change once you confirm;
  • routines: work it does every day, every week, or on every new model version;
  • memory: notes it keeps about how the team works, which anyone can read and edit;
  • a character: a shape, a colour and eyes, so you know it at a glance.

Bots is in the project’s page rail, under Assistant. It belongs to the Assistant module.

What a bot is doing

Section titled “What a bot is doing”

The character moves with the bot’s status, and the list shows its last line of news:

Status The character When
Ready bobs and looks around Nothing to do.
Working bounces, with a turning cog It is on a request or a routine.
Waiting for you hops, with a ring and ! It has prepared changes for you to confirm: 2 for your OK.
Done jumps, with a tick It finished. The line is the first sentence of its answer.
Stuck slumps, with ? Something failed. The line says what.

If you prefer reduced motion in your system settings, the characters stay still.

Hand a bot some work

Section titled “Hand a bot some work”

  1. Open Bots and pick the bot in the list.

  2. Ask in its conversation, or press one of its starter questions: Which RFIs are due this week?

  3. The bot works with its own tools, in this project only, and answers. What it looked at is shown under the answer, as in any conversation with the assistant.

  4. If it prepared a change (a comment, a reminder, an issue), the change is on a card. Check it and press the button to confirm it. Until then, nothing changes.

A bot keeps its conversations together, apart from the project assistant’s. Each person sees the conversations they had with it, and the ones its routines started for them.

Start a bot

Section titled “Start a bot”

Press a template card, or From scratch. Everything a template brings can be changed before the bot is added.

Template Its job Routine
Chief The brief and the team’s work moving Mondays at 07:30
RFI Chaser Answers from the documents, RFIs chased before they go overdue Every day at 08:00
Site Diary The day written up from your notes and photos Every day at 17:00
Clash Triage New clashes grouped, owners proposed, issues drafted On every new model version
Doc Control Names, revisions and packages checked against the standard Mondays at 09:00
Safety Hazards found in the diary, photos and issues Every day at 18:00
Line Planner The work by chainage, on roads and railways Thursdays at 15:00
Snag Chaser Snags tracked to close-out before handover Every day at 08:00

The editor has four parts:

  • Job: the bot’s name, a one-line job shown under its name, and its instructions. Write the instructions as you would brief a new colleague: what to look at, what good looks like, who to ask.
  • Tools: tick what it may use, in three groups.
    • Look things up: issues, RFIs, clashes, files, photos, the diary, elements in the models.
    • Prepare for you to finish: draft an issue, an RFI or a diary entry for you to finish.
    • Change the project · asks first: comment, assign, change a status. Each of these waits on a card.
  • Routines: an instruction it runs every day at a time, every week on a day, or on every new model version. A new routine starts at its next time, not straight away.
  • Memory: notes the bot uses in every conversation. It adds its own when you tell it something to keep, remember that overdue issues go to the site manager first, and you can change or remove any of them.

The preview shows the character. Pick a shape, a colour and eyes, and press a status to see how it moves.

What a bot may do

Section titled “What a bot may do”

A bot never has more access than the person it works for.

  • In a conversation it acts as the person talking to it. It sees what they can see.
  • A routine acts as the person who set it up, and its answer arrives as a notification from the bot.
  • Its tools only. A tool it was not given is refused by AECX, whatever the conversation says. Asked for something outside its job, it says so.
  • Its project only. A bot works in the project it belongs to.
  • Every change is confirmed by a person on a card, as with the assistant’s confirmable changes.
  • Who manages bots: Coordinators and Admins add, change and remove them. Everyone in the project can talk to them.
  • On record: adding, changing and removing a bot is in the audit log, and its work is in the assistant’s activity record like any other answer.

Tip

Give a bot the smallest set of tools its job needs. A Doc Control bot needs the file tools, not the clash tools, and its answers stay on its job.

Bots and your plan

Section titled “Bots and your plan”

Bots come with the Assistant module:

Billing Bots included
Project size 3
Business size 10
Per project 2 for each project with the Assistant

An organisation admin can add extra bots on the Plan page, at £12 a month each. Each extra bot brings 300 assistant credits a month for its routines. A bot’s answers use the organisation’s assistant credits like any other.
